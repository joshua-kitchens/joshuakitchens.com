<p>"Information Access and Informed Society - Digital Citizenship, Information Overload &amp; Political Literacy" is the title of the presentation I'm giving next week at Georgia College's Global Citizenship symposium. As you can probably tell, it's kind of dense topic that I will have to be careful how I unpack it. I didn't exactly come up with it, but it's on the programs, so i'm "running" with it. So far my thoughts on how to break things down look a bit like this. It should be fun</p>
<p>Intro</p>
<p>    Library's perspective on information access<br />
    What we hope to accomplish through collection, organization and access</p>
<p>Accessing Information</p>
<p>    What does it mean to access info?<br />
    How is this complicated today?<br />
    What's the difference with media today versus yesterday</p>
<p>What does literacy mean?</p>
<p>    Many different literacy<br />
    How reading is not actually enough<br />
    Visual, Auditory, Technical, etc, etc (mention transliteracy?)</p>
<p>Organization and Quality of Information</p>
<p>     Web doesn't so much organizes it as jumbles it together<br />
    Is there such thing as information overload?<br />
    Curation of information and the problem with social networks?</p>
<p>Limiting Access to information</p>
<p>    The problem technological inequality<br />
    Expectations of informed public and realties of that public (broadband speeds?)</p>
